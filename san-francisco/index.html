When Your System Fails in San Francisco

If your system quits, how fast help shows up matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep scheduling flexible across the area and answer every call with a live person. Urgent calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat no-heat and no-cool calls as top priority.

The emergency calls we run usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's warm air from the vents, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. And any time of year, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, plugged condensate drains backing water into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have quit. Because our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, the majority of these calls are wrapped up in one visit, at a price you approve before we start.

A little caution before we arrive can prevent a much bigger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be turned off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen risks an overflowing drain pan and a damaged compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us immediately.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in San Francisco

Many mid-season breakdowns trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Swap your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and starting to struggle, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor left alone for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
